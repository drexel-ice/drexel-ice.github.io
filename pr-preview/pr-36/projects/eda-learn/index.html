EDA-Learn: Open and Reproducible Machine Learning for Electronic Design Automation

Learned models that predict expensive late-stage chip metrics from early design stages, and the open schema, dataset, and benchmarks that make machine learning for design automation reproducible.

Digital physical design runs as a chain of stages — synthesis, floorplanning, placement, clock tree synthesis, routing, signoff. Timing, power, area and routability are only measured accurately at the end. Miss a target and the flow runs again, at hours to days per pass.

EDA-Learn is an open, reproducible framework for predicting those late-stage outcomes from early-stage information — and for making such predictions comparable between research groups.

The prediction problem

  • Given the information that exists at a source stage — say, global placement.
  • Predict a quantity measured only at a later target stage — say, post-route wirelength.
  • Beat the tool’s own analytical estimate for that quantity: wireload models, half-perimeter wirelength, Steiner trees, congestion heuristics.
  • Score both against the real target-stage measurement, once the flow completes.
Conventional early estimation and machine learning prediction of a downstream metric. Information available at an earlier source stage feeds both a conventional estimator and a learned predictor; each estimate is evaluated against the target-stage measurement, which is ground truth for scoring rather than an input at inference time.

Target-stage measurements are kept strictly out of the inputs. That is what prevents downstream information from leaking into an early predictor and producing accuracy that would not survive real use.

The closed-data problem

Learned prediction has been demonstrated repeatedly across power, wirelength, timing, parasitics, routability and power integrity. The published results still cannot be reproduced or ranked against one another. Across a survey of fifteen representative studies:

   
10 of 15 depend on commercial implementation tools
6 of 15 report on a proprietary or unnamed process technology
12 distinct network architectures across the fifteen studies
no two share a circuit set, a technology, a toolchain and an evaluation protocol

Three consequences follow:

  • Accuracy figures drawn from different circuits, nodes and toolchains are not comparable.
  • The contribution of a representation or an architecture to a reported number cannot be isolated.
  • A model trained on confidential data cannot be rerun outside the group that built it.

Open tools and datasets already exist — OpenROAD, CircuitNet, CircuitOps. The gap is not raw openness. It is that the design data, the evaluation procedure and the model configuration are each specified separately, so nothing downstream is attributable. This is a specification problem, not a modeling problem.

EDA-Learn

Four layers, each defined on top of the one before it, so that a reported result is attributable to a representation, a configuration or a search procedure rather than to the data it happened to be trained on.

The four layers. Each one is defined on top of the layer before it and releases its own artifacts, so a reported result can be traced to a representation, a configuration or a search procedure rather than to the data it happened to be trained on.

The program began with graph neural networks for gate arrival time and interconnect parasitic prediction, unified into the task-agnostic EDA-ML framework. EDA-Schema and EDA-OpenML generalize that recipe onto open data and an open evaluation protocol. EDA-AutoML and the Neural Architect Agent are in progress; results will be posted as they are published.

EDA-Schema — the open foundation

A stage-aware property-graph data model for digital physical design, a large dataset generated entirely with open tools, and twelve standardized prediction tasks.

From design space to benchmark. Parameter sweeps over eighteen circuits and four open PDKs are run through the OpenROAD flow; every stage's artifacts are parsed into one stage-aware property graph carrying graph, image and heatmap representations; twelve prediction tasks are defined on top, each scored against the EDA tool's own estimate under leave-one-circuit-out evaluation.
   
Design instances 7,776 (1,944 per PDK)
Benchmark circuits 18 — 16 from IWLS’05, plus OpenCores and IBEX
Process design kits Nangate 45 nm, SkyWater 130 nm, IHP SG13G2 130 nm, ASAP 7 nm
Modalities Circuit graphs, spatial layout images, scalar heatmaps, QoR metrics
Benchmark tasks 12, from whole-circuit area and power down to per-cell-arc delay
Evaluation Leave-one-circuit-out, against the EDA tool’s own estimate
  • Parsed from standard formats (LEF/DEF, SPEF, Liberty, STA reports), so it stays compatible with commercial flows.
  • Every attribute records the stage at which it becomes available, making the information boundary of a task part of its definition.
  • Sweeps include barely-passing and barely-failing operating points, not only comfortable ones.
  • Carried toward industry adoption through the Si2 Schema and Si2 LBC Extend & Expand working groups.

EDA-OpenML — reproducible neural prediction

An open-source, open-weight framework in which the prediction problem and the model are specified separately, and the model is an executable configuration rather than a prose description: input features, architecture, hyperparameters, preprocessing, evaluation settings.

A single Fixed MLP — one architecture, one training configuration — is then run across every task, PDK and source stage as a controlled reference rather than a tuned model.

Across 184 prediction conditions  
Lower fold-averaged error than the EDA tool’s own estimate 88
Better on a majority of eighteen held-out circuits 81
Better on all eighteen held-out circuits 15
NG45 interconnect length, global placement → detailed routing MAE 4.69 → 3.39 µm (−27.7%), R² 0.735 → 0.887
  • Interconnect length is the strongest case: favorable in all twelve PDK-stage conditions.
  • No single configuration transfers uniformly — total negative slack, total power and cell-arc prediction are not consistently favorable. Reported, not aggregated away.
  • Data efficiency is measured on two separate axes, utilization (how much existing data is used) and acquisition (how many design runs are generated at all). Accuracy frequently approaches the full-data result on substantially less of both.
